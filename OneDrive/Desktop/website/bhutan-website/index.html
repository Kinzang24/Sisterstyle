about

A kingdom that opened slowly, on its own terms

Bhutan is a small Himalayan kingdom wedged between India and China, known for closely guarding its culture and environment while opening carefully to the outside world. Rather than measuring progress by GDP alone, Bhutan pioneered Gross National Happiness as a guiding philosophy, balancing economic growth with cultural preservation, environmental protection, and good governance.

geography

From subtropical plains to unclimbed summits

Bhutan's landscape rises from subtropical plains in the south to snow-capped Himalayan peaks in the north, including Gangkhar Puensum, one of the highest unclimbed mountains in the world. Dense forests cover over 70% of the country, which the constitution requires to remain under permanent forest cover.

culture

Life shaped by dzongs, prayer flags, and festival

Buddhism shapes daily life in Bhutan, visible in its dzongs (fortress-monasteries), prayer flags, and festivals known as tshechus. Traditional dress — the gho for men and kira for women — is still worn widely, and architecture, art, and craftsmanship follow centuries-old conventions protected by law.
